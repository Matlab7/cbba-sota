## 1. Decisions

Setup of the main analysis:
- design: 4 primary settings × 20 dev instances × CRN seeds 0-1 × {F1-R2, F3-pf0.2} × ρ ∈ {2, 1, 0.5}; 80 instance clusters;
- arms: six T2 arms plus FULL and DS;
- leases: each arm and ρ uses the lease frozen in advance (Section 6);
- value: makespan with failures scored 200 (spec 9.1 sensitivity; success is reported separately);
- statistics: the E3a test is `cbba_sota.dyn.stats.max_regret_test` (simultaneous cluster bootstrap, 10,000 resamples).

| Gate | Rule (spec Section 8) | Result (dev) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **K5** C3 headroom | at ρ = 0.5, the best fixed architecture within 5% of FULL → no C3 method claim | best fixed at ρ = 0.5 is INF-r in both families: **1.853 [1.815, 1.894]** × FULL on F1-R2, **1.794 [1.754, 1.835]** on F3 | **does not fire** (there is ample headroom) |
| **K6** C3 separation | SPARC's worst-case regret not ≥ 2 points below the best fixed architecture's, or DS ≤ SPARC → C3 descriptive | dev-selected variant **V1 = HYB**: max regret **1.192**; best fixed **INF-r 1.006** (SPARC is **18.6 points worse**, not 2 points better); DS (ρ\* = 2) 1.170 < SPARC | **fires on both clauses: C3 is descriptive** |
| **E3a** (preview) | MaxReg(SPARC) < min(MaxReg over A ∪ {DS}), one-sided 95% bound excludes 0 | difference **+0.186**, one-sided 95% upper bound +0.217 (comparator INF-r) | does not hold |
| **K7** lease fragility | any C3 conclusion flips within the lease grid → claim only what holds across it | the K5 and K6 verdicts are the same under all 9 common leases and the tuned set; the ranking conclusions flip (the selected variant V1/V2/V3, the best fixed arm INF-r/REP-clamp/HYB, DS's ρ\*, and SPARC < CEN-F at ρ = 0.5) | **fires for rankings**: only "no headroom kill" and "no SPARC separation" are claimed |

**Reading.**
- There is large headroom under T2: at ρ = 0.5 the fixed architectures are 1.82 (INF-r) to 2.33 (CEN-F) times FULL, pooled over families. So K5 does not stop C3.
- But SPARC does not separate:
  - Its dev-selected variant (V1, which *is* HYB) and its other variants (V2 1.205, V3 1.184) all have larger worst-case regret than INF-r (1.006), REP-clamp (1.170) and DS (1.170).
  - INF-r is the best or within 0.6% of the best arm in every cell. Its outsiders keep only their physically committed head; everything else is re-planned by whoever can reach the robots.
  - The same holds in each setting separately (Section 7.5).
- The spec's K6 consequence is "C3 becomes descriptive, DS is recommended". The dev data do not support recommending DS, which uses CEN-F at ρ ≥ 2 and REP-clamp below (max regret 1.170). **The lowest worst-case regret on dev is the fixed rule INF-r.** This is flagged for the orchestrator (Section 9).
- In the spec's terms this is the "collapse to a simple rule" outcome. The winning rule is "do not honour stale commitments of unreachable robots", S12's mechanism. INF-r applies it at every node, while SPARC keeps station anchoring (CEN-F) inside the station component.

## 2. The C3 world (what was built)

**Driving the env.** `C3Env`, a subclass of `DynTaskEnvX`, is driven through `run_plan` by `C3Controller`, which asks for a wake-up at every comm tick (0.1 = env dt). `third_party` and `env.py` are not modified.

**Per tick** (the protocol of `protocols.py`, now on the real env):
1. Observations are written into the replicas.
2. The frames of the previous tick are delivered (`comm.GossipTransport`: range ρ·r_c(n) with n = robots + 1, GE loss p = 0.2 and ρ_GE = 0.8, one tick per hop, 1500 B per node per tick, CRN stream 5).
3. Leaders whose trigger fired plan with SPARC's planner: 300 iterations, monotone keys, seed = hash(replica digest, tick), κ of the cell's delay model.
4. Every robot adopts the newest route version its own replica holds (4.4).
5. The patient lease runs (4.3, with the absent records of 4.6(a)).
6. The rally rule runs.
7. The frames of this tick are sent.

**Between ticks the env does the physics.**
- Robots depart at the exact finish epoch along their adopted route. They skip only tasks their *own* replica knows are started or finished, so stale knowledge becomes a D2 wasted trip.
- Coalitions start in arrival order.
- LoRR stall calendars, realized durations and failures come from `perturb.realize_instance`, with the same CRN draws as C1-dyn.

**Observation model.**
- The station learns a release at its release time.
- Coalition members observe a start and a finish.
- Surviving co-workers observe an abort at the failure detection and publish `absent(failed robot, task)`.
- A robot at a task site observes that task's status.
- Robots write state records on every change and every 1.0 time units; the station heartbeats too.
- Everything else travels by gossip.

**FULL** is SPARC with ideal comms *in this same world*: instant, lossless joins at every epoch; the lease is the failure detector (h = 5 ticks); the good-comms failure rule; `abandon_on_failure`. FULL is the K5 reference.

**T2 arms** use:
- the patient lease (g, L), tuned per arm and ρ (Section 6);
- rule 4.6 with h_f = 10, silence 2.0, member window 1.5 and membership debounce 0.5;
- `abandon_on_failure = False`: robots waiting elsewhere learn of a failure only through their lease and the replicas.

**Arms** (`protocols.ARMS`). The planner, lease grid, rally, gossip, predictors and triggers are shared by all arms.

| Arm | Station component | Components without the station | Triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEN-F | station plans its members; outsiders' believed routes anchored (their traits count, spec 4.5) | no planning: robots follow their last route, then rally | structural |
| HYB = SPARC-V1 | as CEN-F | leader re-plans every robot on clamped snapshots | every knowledge change + every 1.0 |
| REP-clamp | station re-plans every robot, outsiders clamped | as the station | structural |
| INF-r | station plans its members; outsiders keep only their committed head | leader plans its component only | structural |
| SPARC (V2) | as CEN-F | as HYB | structural |
| SPARC-V3 | as CEN-F | as HYB, gated: plan only if the members' last observed station-component fraction < θ = 0.7, or a member is idle | structural + gate |
| DS | CEN-F if ρ ≥ ρ\*, else REP-clamp; ρ\* chosen on dev (analysis only, built from the CEN-F and REP-clamp rows) | | |

**`C3Env` adds three things the env lacks.** Each is a declared convention and inert without the controller: `test_c3env_without_controller_hooks_equals_dyntaskenvx` gives bit-identical episodes on static, F1-R2 and F3.
1. **Rally (4.3).** An idle robot outside the station component moves toward the station on a straight, stall-aware leg and stops once it hears the station. The next departure starts where it stopped. The station-component test is causal: the robot has not heard the station within the 1.5 member window.
2. **Termination (D7) by a global 1-bit mission-complete signal.** A robot goes home when t ≥ H and every released task is finished. H = 0 cells use the same rule, so a robot that runs out of work waits in place or rallies instead of parking at its depot. The spec's per-replica text of D7 has no termination liveness under partitions (Section 4, defect 1). It remains available as `home_rule="replica"`.
3. **Idle robots are polled at comm ticks.** This replaces the native "decide at your last arrival" fallback, which repeats the same epoch forever once robots may wait after every task is finished.

**Adoption rule.**
- Under T2 (`adopt="keep"`), a robot keeps its current commitment even when a version omits it (spec 4.1: frozen at departure; 4.4: a new version replaces the suffix), and the patient lease resolves it.
- FULL follows the version, as `controller.PlanController` does: a waiting robot abandons a released commitment, and a travelling one leaves on arrival (Section 4, defect 7).
- The choice between "keep" and "follow" for T2 is in Section 5.

## 3. Fidelity and sanity checks

- **FULL against the K0-verified good-comms controller.** This is SPARC-Light through `PlanController` in `DynTaskEnvX`, on the same realizations: MA-AT-25 and SA-BT-50, dev 0-5, seed 0, F1-R2 and F3-pf0.2, 24 pairs, all successful (`c3_full_vs_goodcomms.txt`, final code).
  - GM FULL/controller is 0.995 on F1-R2 and 1.057 on F3.
  - FULL plans at the next tick instead of the event instant.
  - On F3 it pays the termination tail: at H = 0 robots wait in the field and travel home after the last finish.
  - On SA-AT-50 F3 the gap is larger: dev 0, 7 and 18 at seed 0 give 49.8/46.2, 87.3/64.4 and 98.7/69.8. That gap is not the tail; the last finish is 21-27 units later. It is not diagnosed (Section 9).
  - FULL enters only K5, where a slower FULL makes the measured headroom *smaller*. The K5 conclusion is therefore conservative.
- **Connected T2** (ρ = 100: always connected, but one tick per hop, heartbeat 1.0 and GE loss; `c3_connected.txt`; dev 0-3, seed 2, MA-AT-25 and SA-BT-50):
  - F1-R2: every arm is 1.036 [1.002, 1.073] × FULL with GE loss and 1.022 [0.974, 1.083] without loss. The protocol machinery costs 2-4% when nobody is out of range.
  - F3: 1.32-1.59 × FULL, even with full connectivity, and CEN-F, HYB and SPARC succeed in only 7/8. At ρ = 100 those three arms are the same algorithm; they differ from REP-clamp and INF-r only in how a *silent* robot is treated (anchored route, clamped snapshot, head only).
  - So the F3 cost of C3 is mostly rule 4.6 (a silent robot is not treated as failed until h_f = 10 plus evidence), not partitions (Section 8).
- **Tests.** `tests/test_dyn_c3.py`, 17 tests:
  - determinism given the CRN key;
  - under ideal comms, every replica equals global knowledge whenever the planner reads it;
  - the vectorized task kinds equal the replica rule at every sampled tick;
  - the rally leg is stall-aware and the next leg starts where it stopped;
  - the termination rule;
  - rule 4.6(c) (unit tests and episode regressions);
  - the lease absent rule;
  - the FULL deadlock regression;
  - the analysis rules (regret, DS, variant tie rule);
  - the test split is refused.

  `tests/test_dyn_comm.py` gains the 4.6(c) and absent-supersession unit tests.

  The full suite passes: `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/ -q` gives 333 passed in 639 s, and `ruff check cbba_sota scripts tests` is clean.

## 4. Defects found on dev, fixed before the runs they would affect

F3 under C3 had never been piloted (spec Section 1). The pilots used instant flooding within a component, records every tick and a hindsight makespan. On the real env with T2, the day-1 rules had liveness holes. Each one below was found on a dev episode, fixed, and regression-tested.
- Defects 1-6 were fixed before the lease grid.
- Defect 7 was found in the evaluation (FULL only). FULL was rerun; the T2 rows are unaffected (identity check above).

| # | Symptom (dev episode) | Cause | Fix (declared C3 convention) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SA-BT-50 dev 1, seed 2, F1-R2, CEN-F ρ = 0.5: every task finished at 34.4, episode ran to the 200 cap | per-replica D7: the crew of the last task knew everything was finished and went home out of range; nobody else ever learned it | termination by a global mission-complete signal (the env's D7 rule), also at H = 0 |
| 2 | SA-BT-50 dev 0, seed 0, F3: task stuck "started" forever | a robot failing while working alone leaves no witness; the ABORT fact had no observer | FULL: the abort is observed globally (detector). T2: rule 4.6(c), where a START overdue by h_f with a silent crew counts as aborted |
| 3 | SA-BT-50 dev 0, seed 0, F3, SPARC ρ = 1: task 38 never served | 3 robots died en route; 4.6(b) keeps a failed robot on its committed head, and nobody waited there | 4.6(c): an overdue arrival or finish promised by a silent robot counts as an absent record and as failure evidence |
| 4 | SA-BT-50 dev 1, seed 2, F3, CEN-F ρ = 1: 770 abandons, cap reached | the lease named only non-failed members absent, so dead heads stayed in the cover and robots were sent back to wait for them | the lease names every silent planned member absent, declared-failed ones included |
| 5 | MA-AT-25 dev 1, seed 2, F3, CEN-F ρ = 0.5: 430 "no partner" abandons at one task | absent records were permanent: a member re-assigned to the task never counted as coming again | an absent record is superseded by a newer record of the member heading there or a newer route version containing the task |
| 6 | SA-BT-50 dev 2, seed 2, F3, REP-clamp ρ = 100: never finishes | a dead lone worker was never declared failed; clamped planning kept giving its reopened task back to it | the 4.6(c) evidence includes an overdue WORK record |
| 7 | FULL, SA-AT-50 dev 18, seed 0, F3: 30% completion, all 36 live robots waiting | the planner's G4 fallback released a residual's frozen members; a robot that kept waiting at the released task cross-waited with partners of its next task (detector lease: forever) | FULL follows the version (PlanController's rule). T2 keeps the commitment (Section 5); its patient lease breaks such cycles |

**Known residual hole.** A robot that dies while *idle* makes no promise that can become overdue. Clamped planning (REP-clamp, and the stranded leaders of HYB and SPARC) can then give it solo work that nobody does until a coalition partner's lease exposes it. This is not fixed. T2 failures concentrate on F3: 422 of 5,760 T2 episodes failed, 364 of them on SA-AT-50. That is consistent with this hole and with cross-waits under frozen commitments, but it was not diagnosed further (Section 9). Failures are scored 200 and reported separately (Section 7.6).

"keep" (spec-literal) was chosen for T2. "follow" produces heavy churn when conflicting leaders' versions flip robots (up to 573 releases per episode on SA-AT).

## 8. Diagnosis: the C3 section as a benchmark finding

1. **The headroom is real and large.** At ρ = 0.5 the best fixed architecture is about 1.8 times FULL, and CEN-F is 2.3 times FULL (pooled over families). C3 is a hard regime under T2.
2. **On F3, failure handling costs more than partitions.** With full connectivity (ρ = 100), T2 still loses 32-59% on F3 against 2-4% on F1. Rule 4.6 treats a silent robot as unreachable until h_f = 10 plus evidence, and the arms differ mainly in what they do with the silent robot's commitments.
3. **Honouring the stale commitments of unreachable robots is the costly ingredient.**
   - The arms that anchor outsiders' routes at the station (CEN-F, and HYB, SPARC and V3 inside the station component) have the highest regret on F3 and the lowest success.
   - REP-clamp re-plans outsiders on clamped snapshots and is second.
   - INF-r keeps only their physically committed head and re-plans the rest. It is best or tied in every cell, paying in duplicate service (wasted trips).
   - This is S12's mechanism statement, and it collapses the C3 comparison to that one rule.
4. **Leases and the beacon matter as much as architecture on F1** (Sections 6, 7.4 and 7.9). L = 10 is always harmful. The ranking of the fixed architectures flips within the lease grid (K7).
5. **SPARC's stranded-component variants barely differ** (V1/V2/V3 max regret 1.184-1.205), and V3's θ-gate helps only at ρ = 0.5.

## 9. Caveats and items for the orchestrator

- **Recommendation for C3.** The K6 wording recommends DS, but on dev DS (1.170) is dominated by the fixed rule INF-r (1.006), in every setting. The orchestrator should decide whether the descriptive C3 section recommends INF-r, reports DS as the spec's pre-declared recommendation, or both.
- **Conventions added or changed here** (all declared in the module docstrings and to be frozen with the spec):
  - the global mission-complete termination signal;
  - rule 4.6(c) (overdue work, arrival or finish of a silent robot);
  - absent-record supersession;
  - absent records for declared-failed planned members;
  - T2 "keep" versus FULL "follow" adoption;
  - rally stall-aware, heard-based, stopping when the station is heard;
  - idle robots polled at ticks.
- **FULL fidelity.** FULL matches the good-comms controller on MA-AT-25 and SA-BT-50 (0.995 F1-R2, 1.057 F3 with the declared termination tail). On SA-AT-50 F3 it is 8-41% slower on 3 checked episodes, for an undiagnosed planning-side reason (FULL plans about twice as often). This only makes K5 more conservative.
- **Residual liveness hole** (a dead idle robot given solo work) and **concentrated T2 failures** on SA-AT-50 F3 (364 of 422), not diagnosed. A both-succeed sensitivity of E3a was not run.
- **Tuning scope.** Leases were tuned on MA-AT-25 and SA-BT-50 (seed 2, 5 instances) and applied to all four settings. The K7 grid read has only 10 clusters.
- **ρ ∈ {1.25, 0.75}** were run only on the first two settings (Section 7.10, where the five-level decisions are identical). The four-setting gates use ρ ∈ {2, 1, 0.5}.
- **Beacon factor** on 10 clusters per cell, descriptive.
- **Not run:**
  - RL-dec, CBTA-dec and the oracle arms (day-6 items);
  - the T4 decision-difference log (the runner supports it: `ProtocolRunner(shadow=...)`);
  - the corner-station and Bernoulli/Rayleigh channel sensitivities.
- **Budgets.** The per-node byte budget (1500 B per tick), byte model and digest size are conventions. They are the same for every arm.
- **Compute.** CPU per planner call (median per-episode p50 5.7-18.7 ms at 50 tasks) was measured on the loaded shared host and is descriptive. At ρ = 0.5, the replicated arms (HYB, REP-clamp, SPARC, V3) plan 1,569-2,626 times per episode, INF-r 1,204-1,508 and CEN-F 240-273.
