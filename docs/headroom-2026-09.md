# Headroom and anytime study on the validation split (2026-09-28)

Every number below is an env-replayed makespan (RL: its own rollout) from runs made in this study on the new
validation split `val`, which nobody has tuned on. Tables are generated by `docs/results/phase1b/headroom.sh`.

## Summary

- **Split.** `val` (seed base 900000, 20 per generated setting) was generated with the test/dev generator. All 1490
  instance files of test, dev and val have distinct data fingerprints.
- **Measured.** 6 of the 8 H1 settings plus three 20-task settings, val 0-9. The two 500-task settings could not be
  measured cleanly on the shared host in the window.
- **C1 dry run on val (8 cores at B1).** ALNS v2 is 0.4-1.4% above the BKS. CP-SAT LNS is 5.6-14.5% above,
  constructor restarts 7.9-14.6% and RL(s.N) 29-40%. ALNS v2 won every paired instance against all three competitors
  on all 6 H1 settings:
  - / CP-SAT LNS: 0.882-0.953;
  - / RL: 0.716-0.787;
  - / constructor restarts: 0.882-0.933.
  With every row kept (n = 10 per setting; see Caveats), the wins are 10-0 everywhere (RL on SA-BT-25-5-50: 8-0,
  two RL runs were late) and Holm-corrected p <= 4e-5 for every setting x competitor.
- **Headroom (gap to the best of our own runs).** The BKS is almost entirely ALNS output (80 of 90 plans; every
  50+ task BKS), so this is not an independent reference and "gap closed" is not a headline claim. At B1 on 8 cores,
  ALNS v2 closes 86-95% of the gap between our constructor (same budget and cores) and that reference. CP-SAT LNS
  closes -17% to +43%, i.e. it is usually no better than restarting the constructor on the same 8 cores; note that
  it used only about 53% of its 8 cores (single-threaded model building), so it is a weak parallel baseline. RL ends
  2.7-4x further from the BKS than the constructor.
  - The BKS itself is mostly ALNS v2's own best over several runs.
  - A 10-min 16-worker ALNS run (the length the task specified) did not improve it (+2.26% and +0.24% on 2
    instances). 16 independent workers improved it by 0.25% and 0.10% on two settings.
  - The certified CP-SAT bounds are tight only on SA-BT 20-task instances (10/10 proven optimal). On SA-AT/MA-AT
    20-task instances they are 28-40% below the BKS.
  - So the remaining headroom of ALNS v2 at B1 is about 0.4-1.4% to the best known. The distance to the true optimum
    is unknown and probably larger.
- **C2 (1 core, low budget).** ALNS v2 on 1 core is 3.2-8.9% above the BKS at 1 s and 3.9-8.0% at 2 s.
  - At 2 s on 1 core against CP-SAT LNS on 8 cores at B1, the ratio is 0.930-0.982 on 5 of 6 H1 settings. It
    ties on SA-AT-50-5-50 (0.988 [0.975, 1.002]). With all rows kept, 5 of 6 are significant after Holm; in the
    main tables, 4 of 6 (SA-BT-50-5-50 has p_holm = 0.11 with 8 pairs, 6-2).
  - It beats RL(s.N) on 8 cores at B1 on all 6 (0.765-0.827, every pair won, p_holm < 0.002).
  - **It does not beat 8-core constructor restarts at B1 everywhere:** on SA-AT-50-5-50 the ratio is 1.015
    [1.001, 1.028] in the main table (1.003 [0.992, 1.015] with all rows), i.e. C2 fails against that competitor
    there at 2 s, and at 1 s it is 1.014 [1.005, 1.021]; on MA-AT-25-5-50 at 1 s it is not significant
    (p_holm 0.26).
  - In this harness RL produces no solution within 2 s on 50-task instances on CPU because its first lockstep batch
    is 4 samples (3-8 s). A single rollout takes 1.1-2.7 s on one pinned core and scores about 42.8 against about 27
    for ALNS, so the conclusion is unchanged.
  - At 0.5-2 s on 1 core, ALNS v2 closes 7-77% of the constructor-to-BKS gap, least on SA-AT-50-5-50.
- **ALNS v2 vs v1** (the Phase 1 configuration, on val). At B1 on 8 cores, all rows kept:
  - v2/v1 = 0.912 [0.890, 0.933], 10-0, p_holm = 2e-4, on the 200-task setting;
  - 0.982 (p_holm 0.023) on SA-AT-50-5-50 and 0.987 (0.037) on SA-BT-25-5-50;
  - no difference on the others. The main tables give the same picture (200 tasks 0.917, 8-0).
  On 1 core at 1 s, v1 is far behind: 18.0% vs 8.1% above the BKS on SA-AT-50-5-50, 28.6% vs 8.9% on 200 tasks.

## Setup

**Validation split `val`.** Twenty new instances per generated setting (16 settings, 320 instances). They use the
test/dev generator with seeds `900000 + 1000 * setting_index + i`, added to `configs.SPLITS` as `"val": (900_000, 20)`.
`scripts/gen_instances.py --splits val --check` wrote the 320 pickles. It then checked all 1490 instance files
(test, dev, val and RALTestSet): 1490 distinct data fingerprints, 0 clashes, and every generated file equal to a
fresh generation from its seed. Nobody has tuned anything on `val`. In this study it is used only for measurement
(BKS, anytime curves); no ALNS or baseline setting was changed after looking at it.

**Settings and instances.** These are 6 of the 8 prereg H1 settings (SA-BT-25-5-50, SA-BT-50-5-50, SA-AT-50-5-50,
MA-AT-25-5-50, MA-AT-50-5-50, MA-AT-50-5-200) plus three 20-task settings, one per family (SA-BT-25-5-20,
SA-AT-25-5-20, MA-AT-25-5-20). Instances are val 0-9 (10 per setting). The other two H1 settings, the 500-task
settings are not measured (see Deviations).

**Methods** (`scripts/anytime.py`, one row per method, core count and budget):

| Label | Method |
| --- | --- |
| ALNS2 | ALNS v2, the default `ALNSConfig()`: 1 worker, or 8 forked workers with incumbent exchange; seed 0 |
| ALNS1 | ALNS v1 (`ALNSConfig.v1()`) on the same kernels: 1 worker at every budget, 8 workers at B1 |
| CPSAT | CP-SAT LNS (`cpsat.solve_lns`) with 1 or 8 threads, from the `greedy.construct` hint built in min(10% of the budget, 3 s); 2-s sub-solves (10 s on MA-AT-150-10-500, the better variant on dev) |
| RL | RL(s.N), the released policy on 1 or 8 single-threaded CPU processes; each samples lockstep batches until the budget is used (`rl.sample_until`); scored by the best rollout's own env run. RL on 8 cores runs up to B1 only |
| CONSTRUCT | Our regret-insertion constructor with randomized restarts (the plan sequence of `greedy.construct`). 8 independent restart streams (seeds 0-7) run on 8 cores up to the largest budget. CONSTRUCT-1 at budget b is stream 0's best plan finished by b; CONSTRUCT-8 is the best over the 8 streams |

**Budgets and grid.** The budgets are 0.5, 1, 2, 5 and 10 s, B1 (the paper's RL(s.10) time: 6.5-29.8 s for 20 and
50 tasks, 75.2 s for 200 tasks) and 2B1.
- Every instance (val 0-9) gets the core points: ALNS2, ALNS1, CPSAT and RL on 1 core at 0.5, 1, 2 s and B1 and on
  8 cores at B1; ALNS2 (and CPSAT up to 50 tasks) on 8 cores at 2B1; and the 8 restart streams.
- Val 0-4 also get the extended points: every method on 1 core at 5, 10 s and 2B1, and ALNS2, CPSAT and RL on
  8 cores at 0.5-10 s.
- The 20-task settings, which are descriptive only, use 8 cores at B1 only.

A budget starts after the instance and its travel matrices are loaded; construction counts inside it. A run is
**late** if its wall time exceeds the budget by more than 10% + 0.25 s. A late run has no solution within the budget
and is left out of that cell. A failed plan would count as 200; none failed.

**Scoring.** Every makespan is env ground truth: plans are replayed with `pre_set_route` + `execute_by_route`, and RL
uses its own rollout. Rows carry routes, the instance fingerprint, the code version, CPU affinity, load and the
cgroup's throttling counters.

**Execution and host conditions.** Four or five lanes of 8 warm single-threaded processes (the `compare_dev.py`
lanes) are each pinned to 8 idle physical cores. A lane runs one 8-core job, or a bundle of up to 8 one-core jobs with the same
budget. The container shares a 96-CPU quota with other tenants' jobs. Twice during the study a 180-process job of
another project saturated the quota, and every process was throttled in all periods: 254 runnable tasks, and the BKS
ALNS runs did 4-5x fewer iterations. The pinning gives each lane its own cores but no protection from quota
throttling. Therefore:
- a lane starts a unit only when at most 40% of the cgroup's CPU periods were throttled over the preceding 1 s;
- a row whose job saw more than 40% throttled periods is **disturbed**: it is left out of every table and re-run.

A throttled period costs a job only the rest of that period after the quota ran out. ALNS and RL jobs in the kept
rows got 95-98% of their cores on average (5th percentile 83-93%, per setting), about the same for every method. An
earlier 20% cutoff threw away half of all rows, which on this host made the campaign infeasible in the time window.
One pass re-ran the disturbed rows, but the host stayed saturated and almost all re-runs were disturbed again, so
some cells have fewer runs than planned.
Jobs of all methods are shuffled into the same lanes, so methods share host conditions. Rows record the CPU they
received (`cpu_s`), the throttling counters and the load. This study used at most 56 CPUs at any time: 32-40 in
timed lanes, 16 in a BKS lane. The BKS lane was paused for part of the timed campaign to lower contention.

**Best-known solutions** (`scripts/bks.py`) are the best env-replayed plan per instance over:
- the long BKS runs:
  - ALNS v2 with 16 workers (seeds 1000-1015) for T = 30 s (20 tasks), 90 s (50) or 240 s (200);
  - CP-SAT LNS with 8 threads from the construct hint for 90 s (50-task settings, val 0-4);
  - the full CP-SAT model with 8 threads for at most 60 s (20-task settings), hinted with the best plan so far;
- every anytime run on the instance (all methods, budgets and core counts).

Two checks are compared with the BKS but kept out of it, so that every instance's BKS comes from the same
portfolio:
- ALNS v2 with 16 independent workers (no incumbent exchange) for 90 s on val 0-2 of the 50-task settings;
- the length the task specified, 16 workers x 600 s, on val 0 of SA-AT-50-5-50 and MA-AT-50-5-50.

The full model rounds each arc weight up to 1/1000. A critical chain has at most T + 1 arcs, so
`bound - (T + 1)/1000` is a certified lower bound on the true optimum. The BKS plans (routes) are stored in
`runs/bks/val_bks.json` and, versioned, in `docs/results/phase1b/bks_val_plans.json.gz`.

## Deviations from the task, and why

The task asked for these BKS runs: ALNS v2 with 16 workers for 10 min (500 tasks: 20 min) plus CP-SAT LNS with 16
workers for the same time, on every val instance of 11 settings. It also asked for anytime runs of 5 methods at
7 budgets on 1 and 8 cores. At 20 instances this is about 1400 core-hours for the BKS alone; at 10 instances about
700. The window was about 6 hours on at most 64 CPUs, i.e. 384 core-hours, and the host was shared. So:
- **Instances:** val 0-9 per setting, not 20. The extended budget points use val 0-4.
- **BKS budgets:** ALNS v2 with 16 workers for 30/90/240 s (20/50/200 tasks) instead of 10 min. CP-SAT LNS with
  8 threads for 90 s on val 0-4 of the 50-task settings only. Two checks were added: ALNS v2 with 16 independent
  workers (no exchange) for 90 s on val 0-2 of the 50-task settings, and the specified 10 min x 16 workers on one
  instance each of SA-AT-50-5-50 and MA-AT-50-5-50. The check runs are compared with the BKS and kept out of it.
- **500-task settings** (MA-AT-150-10-500, MA-AT-150-5-500) have no timed val runs. A B1 job there takes
  560 s x 8 cores. The first such jobs ran through a burst of another tenant's 180-process job and got 58% of their
  cores; with the host's recurring bursts they could not be measured cleanly in the window. The prereg C1 dry run
  for these two settings is still open. Dev-split numbers are in `docs/results/alns-v2/`.
- **Grid** as in Setup: RL on 8 cores up to B1 only; ALNS1 on 8 cores at B1 only; on 200 tasks, 2B1 on 8 cores for
  ALNS2 only. RL(s.N) runs on CPU (lockstep), not GPU.

## Caveats

- **Shared, throttled host.** The container's 96-CPU quota was saturated for long stretches by other tenants'
  jobs. The main tables drop disturbed rows (>40% throttled CPU periods during the job). Kept rows got 95-98% of
  their cores on average (ALNS and RL jobs, 5th percentile 83-93%). Some cells therefore have fewer than the
  planned runs, and paired ratios use the pairs present (`n` in the CSV, wins-losses in the tables). The
  sensitivity report keeps every row (`anytime_val_all.txt/.md`; CPU received mean 93-96%, 5th percentile 71-85%)
  and reaches the same conclusions: same signs, ratios within about 0.02. Absolute budgets on this host mean
  slightly less CPU than on a quiet host, for every method alike.
- **The BKS is a best-known value, not an optimum.** Beyond the proven 20-task SA-BT instances, certified bounds
  are weak: the CP-SAT bound is 28-40% below the BKS on the SA-AT/MA-AT 20-task instances, and no bound was run on
  50+ tasks. Single strong ALNS runs end 0.4-1.5% above the portfolio best, and the independent-worker check still
  improved it on two settings. So the BKS on 50+ tasks is probably not optimal, and how far it is from the optimum
  is unknown. A method's gap to the optimum is at least its gap to the BKS, and its true share of the gap closed
  is at most the reported one. Paired ratios between methods do not depend on the BKS.
- **BKS and ALNS2 share an algorithm.** Most BKS plans come from ALNS v2 runs: the long runs or the 8-core anytime
  runs. The CP-SAT LNS long runs and the CP-SAT full model are independent members, and neither beats the ALNS BKS
  on 50+ tasks (the BKS section above).
- **n = 10** (extended points n = 5), one ALNS seed per (instance, budget). Val instances were never used for
  tuning. The 500-task settings are not measured here.
- **RL on CPU.** RL(s.N) runs the released policy on single-threaded CPU processes, lockstep. On 50 tasks it
  fits 13-21 samples into B1 on 1 core and 108-172 on 8 cores, more than the paper's RL(s.10). Its first lockstep
  batch takes 1-2 s (20 tasks), 3-8 s (50) and 45-66 s (200), so it has no solution below those budgets.

# val split and disjointness check
