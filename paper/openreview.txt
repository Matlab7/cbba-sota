Title:
Lightweight Search Beats Exact Solvers and Learned Policies in Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Task Allocation

Abstract (291 words):
Heterogeneous robot teams must form coalitions whose combined capabilities cover each task's requirements and whose members arrive together before the task can start. This couples coalition formation, routing and scheduling, and learned policies have recently been reported as the state of the art for it. We ask whether that claim survives a comparison at equal compute, and how much compute a well-engineered search needs to win. We present an adaptive large neighbourhood search (ALNS) over a deadlock-free plan representation, a global task order with a minimal covering coalition per task, whose evaluator reproduces the benchmark's simulator exactly and scores an insertion move in time linear in the coalition size. On the HeteroMRTA benchmark, with up to 150 robots and 500 tasks, we compare it with the published reinforcement-learning policy, three methods built on the constraint solver CP-SAT (including a fully parallel large neighbourhood search), the exact MILP the benchmark used (CTAS-D, ported to an open solver), and greedy restarts. The competitors run on 8 pinned cores for the benchmark's own time budgets, and the confirmatory test run follows a pre-registration frozen before it. A fraction of that compute suffices: on one core for 2 s, at most 1.9% of a competitor's CPU time, ALNS is significantly better than every competitor on 7 of the 8 settings and than the learned policy on all 8. At equal compute, it is significantly better than every competitor on all 8 settings, with plans 3.6–13.9% shorter than those of the CP-SAT-based methods and greedy restarts and 21.4–32.8% shorter than those of the learned policy. Making CP-SAT fully parallel does not close the gap, and the exact MILP finds a plan within the budget for only 72 of the 300 instances with up to 200 tasks.

Keywords:
Multi-robot task allocation; Coalition formation; Scheduling with synchronization; Large neighbourhood search; Constraint programming; Benchmarking
