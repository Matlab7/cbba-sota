Title:
Search Beats Learning at Equal Compute: A Pre-Registered Study of Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Coalition Formation and Scheduling

Abstract (264 words):
Heterogeneous robot teams must form coalitions whose combined capabilities cover each task's requirements and whose members arrive together before the task can start. This couples coalition formation, routing and scheduling, and learned policies have recently been reported as the state of the art for it. We ask whether that claim survives a comparison at equal compute. We present an adaptive large neighbourhood search (ALNS) over a deadlock-free plan representation, a global task order with a minimal covering coalition per task, whose evaluator reproduces the benchmark's simulator exactly and scores an insertion move in time linear in the coalition size. On the HeteroMRTA benchmark, with up to 150 robots and 500 tasks, we compare it with the published reinforcement-learning policy, three CP-SAT baselines (including a fully parallel large neighbourhood search), the exact MILP the benchmark used (CTAS-D, ported to an open solver and checked against the original authors' solutions), and constructor restarts. All methods run on the same pinned cores at the benchmark's own time budgets, and the confirmatory test run follows a pre-registration frozen before it. ALNS is significantly better than every competitor on 8 of the eight settings: its plans are 3.2–13.3% shorter than those of the CP-SAT baselines and restarts, and 20.1–32.0% shorter than those of the learned policy, which runs at its published time budget on the same CPUs. Making CP-SAT fully parallel does not close the gap, and the exact MILP rarely finds a plan within the budget. On one core and in 2 s, ALNS is already significantly better than every eight-core competitor on 4 of the eight settings.

Keywords:
Multi-robot task allocation; Coalition formation; Scheduling with synchronization; Large neighbourhood search; Constraint programming; Benchmarking
