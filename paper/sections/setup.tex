\paragraph{Instances.} The benchmark has three families of instances (Section~\ref{sec:problem}): single-skill robots
with binary requirements (called SA-BT in the benchmark paper), single-skill robots with additive requirements (SA-AT)
and multi-skill robots with additive requirements (MA-AT). We study its eight settings with 50 or more tasks
(Table~\ref{tab:settings}), from 25 robots and 50 tasks up to 150 robots and 500 tasks. As pre-registered, the smaller
settings with 20 tasks, which exact methods already handle (CP-SAT proves every validation instance of the smallest
single-skill setting optimal), are only reported in the supplement. For each setting we generate three disjoint sets
of instances with the benchmark's own generator: 20 development instances, on which ALNS and all competitors were
tuned; 20 validation instances, used only for a dry run of the whole protocol and for reference solutions; and 50 test
instances, run once for the confirmatory results. On our test instances the released RL policy reproduces the
published mean makespans within half a standard deviation on all 16 settings of the benchmark, which confirms both the
instances and our use of the policy.

\paragraph{Competitors.} Every competitor is compared with ALNS separately and runs on the same 8 cores.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RL policy}: the released policy of \citet{dai2025heterogeneous}. It builds a plan by simulating the
  mission: whenever a robot becomes free, the policy chooses its next task, so its decisions are event-driven.
  Sampling several such rollouts and keeping the best is the strongest variant of the benchmark paper (ten rollouts);
  we let 8 single-threaded processes sample rollouts until the budget is used, which gives \RLNlo{} to \RLNhi{} times
  as many rollouts as published.
\item \textbf{CP-LNS}: large neighbourhood search that uses the constraint solver CP-SAT \cite{perron2026cpsat} for its
  repair step. Each step frees a window of tasks (along the chain that sets the makespan, close in space or time, or
  at random), keeps all other coalitions fixed and lets CP-SAT re-optimise the freed part with 8 threads, starting
  from our greedy construction.
\item \textbf{Parallel CP-LNS}: the same search as 8 single-threaded copies that share their best plan, so that all
  cores stay busy.
\item \textbf{CP-SAT full model}: CP-SAT's own parallel search \cite{perron2023cpsatlp} on the complete model of the
  problem (one route per robot over the tasks it can serve, coverage constraints, symmetry breaking between identical
  robots), started from the best of 8 greedy constructions.
\item \textbf{CTAS-D}: the exact mixed-integer program of \citet{fu2023robust} that the benchmark paper used. We port
  it from the authors' public code to CP-SAT as the solver (no Gurobi licence) and run it with 8 threads and no warm
  start. The Gurobi solutions shipped with the benchmark's 50 released test instances satisfy our model and reproduce
  their objective values, and on ten of them our port finds, in 60\,s, plans 4.1\% shorter than the shipped 600-s
  Gurobi runs. On the two 500-task settings it found no solution within the budget on the development instances and
  needed 55--87\,GB of memory, so it is counted as failing there and not run.
\item \textbf{Greedy restarts}: 8 independent streams of our randomized greedy constructions
  (Section~\ref{sec:search}); the best plan found within the budget counts.
\end{itemize}
The length of a repair step of the two CP-LNS variants and the arcs of the full model were chosen per setting on the
development instances (supplement). Every plan is scored by replaying it in the benchmark's simulator; the RL policy is
scored by its own rollout. A run without a plan, or with a plan that does not finish all tasks before the simulator's
time limit, counts as makespan 200.

\paragraph{How the methods run.} All methods are used as offline planners, as in the benchmark paper: given the whole
instance, a method computes one plan within its time budget, and the simulator then executes that plan without
re-planning. The budget $B_1$ of a setting is the computation time that the benchmark paper reports for its policy
with ten rollouts (13--560\,s, Table~\ref{tab:settings}); we also report twice that budget, $2B_1$. Except for CP-SAT
full model and CTAS-D, which solve their model once for the whole budget, every method is an anytime loop that repeats
a basic step until the budget ends (Table~\ref{tab:steps}). ALNS makes millions of cheap iterations, each evaluated
exactly; the CP-LNS variants make tens to thousands of solver calls of about a second; greedy restarts make thousands
of constructions; and the RL policy completes tens to a few hundred rollouts, each a simulation of the whole mission.

\paragraph{Compute.} All runs used one host with two Intel Xeon Platinum 8480C processors in a container with a quota
of 57.6 CPUs, running at most six lanes of 8 CPUs, one per physical core, with the jobs of all methods shuffled into
the same lanes. Budgets start once the instance is loaded in a warm process (compiled kernels, loaded network weights)
and include construction and model building. A lane starts a job only when at most 40\% of the container's CPU periods
were throttled in the last second; a job that saw more throttling is re-run. A run that overruns its budget by more
than 10\% + 0.25\,s is scored by the best plan it had found by the budget, or as a failure if it records none. We
report the CPU time that each method actually used.

\paragraph{Hypotheses and analysis.} We pre-registered three hypotheses. \textbf{C1}: in each setting, ALNS on 8 cores
at budget $B_1$ has a lower mean makespan than each competitor on 8 cores at $B_1$ (46 tests: 8 settings $\times$ 6
competitors, without CTAS-D on the 500-task settings). \textbf{C1 at $2B_1$}: the same against parallel CP-LNS, CP-SAT
full model and greedy restarts at twice the budget, on the six settings with up to 200 tasks (17 tests).
\textbf{C2}: ALNS on a single core for 2\,s is better than each competitor on 8 cores at $B_1$ (46 tests). For each
instance we compute the ratio of makespans ALNS / competitor and report its mean with a paired bootstrap 95\%
confidence interval (10,000 resamples) \cite{efron1993introduction}. Each test is a one-sided paired $t$-test of the
mean log ratio below zero, with Holm's correction \cite{holm1979simple} within each of the three hypotheses at
$\alpha = 0.05$. C1 holds in a setting if all its tests are significant, and we claim C1 if it holds in at least six
of the eight settings. There is no minimum effect size; we report effect sizes throughout.

\paragraph{Pre-registration.} Hypotheses, competitors and their parameters, budgets, instances, execution rules,
analysis and decision rule were written down and frozen before any ALNS or CP-SAT run on the test instances. The frozen
document records the git object identifiers of the source code, and our harness refuses to run on the test instances
unless the checked-out code is exactly that code. The test instances were run once. The known weaknesses of the
protocol are listed in the pre-registration and in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. All seeds are fixed: one run per
instance, method and budget.
